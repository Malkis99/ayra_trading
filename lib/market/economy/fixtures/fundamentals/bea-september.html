Personal Income and Outlays, September 2026

EMBARGOED UNTIL RELEASE AT 8:30 a.m. EDT, Thursday, October 29, 2026 BEA 26–46 Personal Income and Outlays, September 2026 Personal income increased $54.5 billion (0.2 percent at a monthly rate) in September, according to estimates released today by the U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA). Disposable personal income (DPI) —personal income less personal current taxes—increased $52.7 billion (0.2 percent), and personal consumption expenditures (PCE) increased $113.1 billion (0.5 percent). Personal outlays —the sum of PCE, personal interest payments, and personal current transfer payments—increased $113.0 billion in September. Personal saving was $911.1 billion, and the personal saving rate —personal saving as a percentage of DPI—was 3.8 percent. The increase in current-dollar personal income in September primarily reflected increases in compensation and government social benefits. The $113.1 billion increase in current-dollar PCE in September reflected increases of $35.1 billion in spending on goods and $78.1 billion in spending on services. Real PCE increased $2.6 billion ( less than 0.1 percent at a monthly rate) in September. From the preceding month, the PCE price index for September increased 0.5 percent . Excluding food and energy, the PCE price index increased 0.3 percent. From the same month one year ago, the PCE price index for September increased 3.6 percent . Excluding food and energy, the PCE price index increased 3.1 percent from one year ago.
